3. Time sinks -- a game idea arises.

June 14, 2025 · 3 min

🧱 Pixel Art: An Unexpected Obsession#

I don’t really know how I got sucked into the world of Aseprite and pixel art so fast, but man — that rabbit hole is dangerous.

While fleshing out my ECS (Entity, Component, System) and rendering systems, I kept thinking: What will this game actually look like? How will it feel? Will it have a narrative? Will it be 2D or 3D?

It’s no secret that making a solo game is hard as hell — especially when you’re not using Blueprints or Unity’s editor. Once programming is under control, all that’s left is art and audio — which, funny enough, are exactly the areas where I have the least natural talent.

My siblings were mini-prodigies with a pencil, but I’m lucky if I can sketch a readable stick figure. Even my handwriting looks like it’s in distress.

So I went on a deep, desperate YouTube dive: What do devs like me do when they suck at art?

Turns out, a lot of us find salvation in pixel art.

🎨 Sprites, Wizards, and Perspective Choices#

I’ve been messing around with Aseprite for a few days now — just drawing random stuff to see if I can produce anything even remotely “shippable.” So far? I’ve created a wizard made up of less than 100 pixels. He’s simple. No detail. But he’s mine.

With art exploration came the next question: what camera perspective should I go for?

First-person is likely off the table — I want to keep this 2D, both to stay sane and to learn SDL/OpenGL fundamentals without firehosing my brain. 3D does tempt me — especially with how much storytelling control you can get from a camera — but for now, I’m leaning toward either:

A top-down RPG-style perspective, or

A platformer/side-scroller (classic indie territory)

Still undecided. But it’s progress just having those narrowed down.

🐢 A Story Device Rooted in Time#

I want anything built in this engine to feel alive. The world should grow, decay, and respond to time. Not just passively — but meaningfully.

And then it hit me: What if everything around the player aged faster?

Like… wildly faster.

It opens up some juicy narrative possibilities. Imagine a childhood friend dying mid-conversation. Or NPCs avoiding you because of the fear you represent. A character cursed (or blessed?) with accelerated entropy in their vicinity.

Except one doesn’t fear you: A turtle.

Turtles are not just adorable — they also exhibit negligible senescence. That means they basically don’t age the same way we do. They don’t deteriorate functionally over time the way humans do. (Whales also qualify, but they don’t fit in a backpack.)

So yeah — the idea of a turtle sidekick has officially entered the design doc. It’s thematic, scientific, and cute.

⚙️ Engine Roadmap: Next Steps#

🧰 Tooling & Debug

Prototype cutscene playback

It’s a lot. But it’s my a lot. And I’m excited.
